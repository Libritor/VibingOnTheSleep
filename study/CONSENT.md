# Information sheet: dream incubation at sleep onset

MannLab Canada and the University of Toronto, Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering.
Investigator: Alexander Vicol. Supervisor: Prof. Steve Mann.
Version 0.1, 27 October 2026. Not yet reviewed by a Research Ethics Board. Until an REB approval
number is on file, only the investigators may take part, as a self-experiment.

## What the study asks

Whether a short spoken prompt, played in the first minute of falling asleep, changes what you
think about in that minute and how many ideas you have afterwards. The idea goes back to Thomas
Edison's naps holding a steel ball, and was tested in the laboratory by Lacaux and colleagues
(2021) and Haar Horowitz and colleagues (2023).

## What happens in one session (about 45 minutes)

1. You list uses for an everyday object for 3 minutes, typing one per line.
2. You sit back in a reclining chair wearing a Muse headband, eyes closed, awake, for 3 minutes.
3. You are then allowed to fall asleep. When the headband detects that you are drifting off, by
   your head nodding or by your brain activity, a recorded voice speaks one sentence.
4. About a minute later a tone wakes you and the voice asks what you were thinking about. You
   answer aloud for up to 45 seconds and your answer is recorded.
5. This repeats up to three times. If the headband sees you entering deeper sleep, it wakes you
   straight away.
6. You list uses for the same object again for 3 minutes.

There are ten sessions on different days. In half of them the sentence mentions the object and in
the other half it does not. You will not be told which is which in advance.

## Risks

Being woken several times can leave you groggy for a few minutes. Do not drive or operate
machinery for 30 minutes after a session. The headband is a consumer product with dry electrodes
and no electrical stimulation. Some people find dream reports personal; you may decline to
describe anything, and you may ask for any recording to be deleted.

Do not take part if you have a diagnosed sleep disorder, epilepsy or a history of seizures, are
taking sedating medication, or are pregnant.

## Your data

- Everything is stored under a pseudonym (for example P-0003) on the investigator's computer.
  Your name is kept only on the signed paper form, separately.
- Recorded: the headband's four EEG channels and accelerometer, the detector's output, the times of
  every prompt and wake, your typed lists, and the audio of your spoken reports.
- Audio is transcribed on the same computer. Transcripts may be scored by a language model if you
  agree; the model sees the text only, never the audio and never your name. You may refuse this
  part and still take part, in which case only people score your transcripts.
- Nothing is published that could identify you. Quotations from reports are used only with your
  separate permission.
- No data go to any website, cloud service other than the scoring model you agreed to, or
  blockchain.

## Withdrawing

You can stop any session at any time by opening your eyes and saying so, and you can leave the
study at any time without giving a reason. You can ask for all of your data to be deleted.

## This is not medical care

The rig is a research instrument, not a medical device, and it does not diagnose or treat anything.
